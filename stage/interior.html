Machine interior options

What you see through the sheet opening. Today prod paints a semi-transparent block with hard edges, the fan of typebars converging right at the top of the opening and the ribbon 74px below that. Two things change here regardless of which background wins: the basket drops so the bars pivot from below the platen the way a real one does, and the ribbon and segment drop with it so the ribbon stays on the bar tips.

The slider moves all three together. 0 is where prod sits today. The well is 400px deep in this rig against prod's 320px, because a dropped basket needs floor under it or the fan runs into the bottom fade.

Paper is off by default so the whole well shows. Turn it on before choosing: in normal use the sheet covers most of this, and an interior that only works with the paper off is the wrong interior.

I1 Painted well quietest; no structure, just depth
  • Surface: japanned black enamel with orange peel, one soft reflection band, light dying out over the top lip.
  • Reads as: the inside of a closed hood. Lets the bars and ribbon carry the whole shot.
  • Risk: with the paper on, the visible strip is almost featureless.
I2 Ribbed casting closest to what prod does now, done properly
  • Surface: vertical ribs with their own specular edges, a rolled top radius catching the light, dust pooling in the bottom.
  • Reads as: formed sheet metal. Holds up at the thin strip either side of the paper, which is where it will mostly be seen.
  • Risk: ribs can moire against the typebar fan at some widths.
I3 Segment shelf most mechanism; frame rails and a cross brace
  • Surface: frame rails down both sides, a lit shelf the segment stands on, a steel cross brace spanning the well, black falling away behind.
  • Reads as: the space under the segment on a real machine.
  • Risk: busiest of the four; the brace competes with the ribbon for attention.
I4 Depth cavity perspective instead of material
  • Surface: side walls raking inward, a lit floor receding to a horizon line, back wall darkest at the join.
  • Reads as: a box you look down into. The bars hang in space rather than sitting on a backdrop.
  • Risk: the perspective is baked into gradients, so it only holds at one opening width.
