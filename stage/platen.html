Platen / carriage upgrade options

Four builds of the same assembly, all showing how the pieces join: a rail with rack teeth under the carriage, end-frame castings the platen shaft runs through, a knob shaft, bail arms pivoting back into the frames, and the return lever braced by a stay. Distress is layered per piece, not one global overlay. Hover a rig to throw the lever.

P1 Machined steel frames, service-worn closest to what is on screen now, just properly assembled
  • End frames with bearing bosses, three screws each, polished rub-through on the front edge.
  • Rail runs past both ends with escapement rack teeth beneath; margin stops ride the scale.
  • Distress: nicks at the casting edges, angled scratch cluster, bruised type-strike band on the roller.
P2 Open brass tie-rod frame most mechanical read: you can see through the machine
  • Two brass tie rods span frame to frame, one above the platen and one below the bail.
  • Brass-over-steel castings with patina, brass line gauge.
  • Distress: pitting in the castings plus ink spatter thrown across the whole assembly.
P3 Enclosed shell with paper table heaviest, most solid; hides the mechanism behind a plate
  • Sloped paper table behind the platen, so the sheet visibly feeds over something.
  • Taller shell-style end frames with a worn-bright top edge.
  • Distress: feed scuffs and streaks across the table where every sheet has dragged.
P4 Black crinkle paint, chipped to steel grimmest and highest contrast against the green desk
  • Crinkle-textured black paint on frames and lever, chrome rail and brass gauge for contrast.
  • Bail and stay darkened so the bright pieces read as the moving parts.
  • Distress: paint chipped back to bare steel at the corners, rust freckles, full-strength roller wear.
