Brass tie-rod carriage - four builds

All four keep the P2 open frame: brass rods spanning frame to frame, patinated castings, brass line gauge, pitting and ink spatter. What changes is the frame detail and, in each case, the carriage return arm, which is now a real linkage: a bracket bolted to the frame, a pivot boss, a notched quadrant the arm sweeps against, and a shaft-elbow-grip group that swings about the boss. Hover a rig for the half-throw, press the arm for the full throw.

Nothing floats. The end castings now run from the rail above the roller down past the bail, and every part lands on one of them: rods end in hex nuts inside the castings, the gauge is bolted across both with visible bolt heads, the bail hangs from arms whose pivot buttons sit on the castings, the feed knob is joined by a shaft, and the return arm's bracket, quadrant and boss are all mounted flat on the left casting.

B1 Swept arm, bakelite ball simplest silhouette; the ball reads instantly as a handle
  • Arm: shaft tapers from the pivot outward, ending in a turned bakelite ball with a thumb-polished wear ring.
  • Frame: two rods, three screws per casting, margin stops on the gauge.
B2 Pierced blade, knurled cross-bar most machined-looking; heaviest hardware read
  • Arm: flat tapered blade with three lightening holes punched through it, ending in a vertical knurled bar you grab across, not a knob.
  • Frame: third mid rod, and the castings themselves are pierced so the desk shows through them.
B3 Dog-leg arm, tube grip most authentic geometry: the grip lies parallel to the platen
  • Arm: shaft bends at an elbow into a second segment, carrying a long bakelite tube grip angled along the platen, chipped at one end.
  • Frame: nickel castings with brass fittings, plus an exposed line-space ratchet wheel outboard on the right.
B4 Light rod, coil spring, thumb paddle most delicate; the spring sells it as sprung, not just decorative
  • Arm: thin brass rod with a visible coil return spring wound around the pivot, ending in a flat thumb paddle with a worn face.
  • Frame: tarnished brass with verdigris creeping out of the pits; darkest, oldest-looking build.
